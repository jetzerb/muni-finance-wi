# Fitchburg Vehicle Registration Fee Notes

## Introduction
Rationale for collecting additional revenue.

While property taxes have risen each year, and the budget has grown each year, inflation has reduced the buying power substantially.

## sfh-tax-awi
- Blue line = Property Tax on Single Family Home over the years, Inflation-Adjusted to Dec 2024 dollars.
  - Read values from the left-hand scale.
  - Information pulled from [City of Fitchburg budgets](https://fitchburgwi.gov/1998/Financial-Records-Reports) 2008-2025.

- Pink/Purple line = National Median Income, Inflation-Adjusted to Dec 2024 dollars.
  - Read values from the right-hand scale.
  - Information pulled from SSA.Gov's [National Average Wage Index](https://www.ssa.gov/OACT/COLA/AWI.html)

- Note that Income has steadily increased since 2011, with one minor COVID dip in 2020.

- Note that the _buying power_ of the SFH Property Tax from 2022 onward has been lower than at any point since we took on the library, and has been decreasing since 2020 while income has risen during that same time.  We'll dive into this further a couple slides from now.

- The lone blue diamond on the right shows what the effect of 2-car VRF (i.e., an extra $80) would have been on the 2024 tax bill.  Even with the VRF, the buying power is still at the lowest point since the library came online.


## sfh-tax-local
- Similar to the last chart.

- Blue is again Property Tax on SFH, adjusted to 2024 dollars.

- Blue diamond is again the effect of adding $80, and still at historic low.

- Pink/Purple line is Fitchburg's Median Adjusted Gross Income.
  - Information taken from WI DOR [Municipal Income Per Return Report](https://www.revenue.wi.gov/Pages/Report/WI-Muni-AGI.aspx)
  - Median AGI is not available for years prior to 2014, and the most recent year is 2023 because as of 2025-04-08 we don't yet have 2024 tax data.

- Again, we see that while Median Income has risen, and leveled off since 2018 except for the COVID dip in 2020, the SFH Property Tax has declined.


## percap-budget-awi
- These next two slides are analogous to the first two, but instead of SFH tax, we're looking at per-capita operating budget.

- Gold Line = Total City Operating Budget, divided by the number of residents, adjusted to Dec 2024 dollars.
  - This is the entire city portion of the tax levy plus all intergovernmental aid.
  - Population information was taken from the WI DOA's [Population and Housing Unit Estimates](https://doa.wi.gov/Pages/LocalGovtsGrants/Population_Estimates.aspx) page -- Time Series 1970-2024 municipalities spreadsheet.

- Gold Diamond is the effect of an extra $1M factored into the levy.  The result is still lower than all but one year since the library.

- Pink/Purple line is again National Median Income

- Similar to the SFH chart, we can see that the _buying power_ of the city's operating budget on a per-capita basis has gone down every year since 2018 while incomes have risen


## percap-budget-local
- Similar to the last chart, but with Fitchburg Median Income rather than National.

- Again, the buying power of the per-capita budget has fallen though the median income remains at the highest recordded inflation-adjusted levels.


## tax-as-pct-of-awi
- Blue Line is SFH Tax divided by National Median Income for each year.
  - No need to adjust for inflation--this is just the percentage of median income that the city's portion of the tax bill represents during each year we have data for.
  - Note that the property tax burden is currently at the lowest point in two decades.

- Gold Line is the Per-Capita budget amount as a fraction of median income.  Again, it's at the lowest point in two decades.

- Blue diamond represents the effect of a household paying 2 VRFs

- Gold diamond represents the effect of the extra $1M VRF in the operating budget.

- Even with the VRF, the tax burden is lower than any point since the library


## tax-as-pct-of-local
- Same thing as the previous chart, but this time the denominator is Fitchburg's Median Household Income.

- Reminder that we only have data for 2014 onward.

- Current tax burden is the lowest it's been in that entire decade-long timeframe.

- And would still be the lowest burden even if we had collected the full $1M VRF


## city-portion
- As we're all aware, the city's levy is a minority of the entire tax bill.

- This chart shows the city's portion of the total levy over time.  We have data only back to 2008.

- City's share has been shrinking since 2018

- Currently lower than any point since the library

- Even with the extra $1M VRF, still lower than any point since the library

## tax-vs-assessed-value
- Every time we have a revaluation year and increased assessments go out, residents get uneasy and worry about their property tax bill for the next half year

- Most residents aren't aware that the tax levy is largely independent of their assessed value, because everyone's assessment goes up by roughly the same amount, so everyone's share of the pie remains fairly constant

- Except, of course, that there _are_ uneven assessment increases for all houses near recent sales, and perhaps other reasons the assessing dept could explain

- Regardless, Blue line = Median SFH property tax by year (nominal dollars, not inflation adjusted)

- Black line = Median SFH assessed value
  - 2005 = first revaluation after many years, approx 60% jump, but the property tax amount was nearly identical
  - 2010 = library.  No change in assessed value, but property taxes jumped 18%
  - 2022 revaluation: assessed value up 15%, property tax down 3%
  - 2024 revaluation: assessed value up 20%, property tax up 3%

## shared-revenue

- Fitchburg's share was over $800,000

- Plot shows all 1850+ municipalities with median AGI on the x axis and per-capita shared revenue on the y axis

- Outliers labeled (overlapping names hard to read)

  - over $800 per resident shared revenue

- Fitchburg roughly $60 per resident and $60k AGI

## Summary
- Current SFH property tax buying power is at a 15 year low

- Current per-capita operating budget buying power is at a 15 year low

- The effect of a $40 VRF is helpful but still leaves us in roughly the same position
